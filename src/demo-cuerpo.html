Taking the writes out of the monolith

The monolith serves client requests, generates reports and runs nightly jobs in the same process and against the same database. A month-end close degrades reads for everyone. This proposal moves writes behind a queue and leaves the read path free.

Today's problem

Everything shares one process: the API that answers clients, report generation and scheduled jobs. None of them can be scaled without scaling the others, and a heavy job competes for the same database connection as a user request.

Proposed architecture

The entry service decides per operation: anything that writes goes to a queue and is applied later; anything that only reads passes straight through to the data service. The scheduler moves into its own piece and stops living inside whatever serves requests.

Only two operations are asynchronous

Create and update go through the queue. Search, download and list stay synchronous and the entry service simply forwards them.

The cache is the expensive part

It sits in the data service, not the entry service, which writes and then reads back to resolve references. Removing it means redoing that part.

Split first, clean up after

One delivery moves the piece as it stands, another removes the cache. Combining them mixes a verifiable change with the only one that can alter results.

What gets removed

Clients stop talking to the monolith and go through the gateway. Once the last report consumer migrates, the monolith, its internal cron and the report generator have no reason to exist: nobody calls them.

Who gets in and with which credential

Three consumers with three different models. Better to settle this before building, because the new piece is either born with the authorization layer inside or it is not born right.

Open decisions

  1. 01

    Is the queue necessary, or is a background job enough?

    This is the highest-impact decision: it determines whether there is one more piece of infrastructure to operate. If the real peak fits in a background process, the queue is unnecessary.

    Does the write return 200 or 202 with a pollable identifier?
